Short Summary:
___________________
The chapter is divided into two parts, both highlighting the power of determination and devotion to music. Part I narrates the inspiring life of Evelyn Glennie, who lost her hearing gradually but refused to let deafness limit her dreams. With guidance from Ron Forbes, she learned to feel music through vibrations across her body and went on to become a world-famous percussionist, performing internationally and inspiring millions, especially the differently-abled. Part II describes the life of Ustad Bismillah Khan, the shehnai maestro who elevated the instrument from temples and weddings to the classical stage. It traces his early life, training under his uncle Ali Bux, his historic performance on India’s Independence Day, and his deep love for Indian culture, Benaras, and the Ganga. Together, both stories celebrate perseverance, cultural heritage, and the triumph of human spirit.

  Poem Name: Wind
  Poet: Subramania Bharati
____________________________________________


Summary:
___________________
The poem addresses the wind as a powerful force that causes destruction by breaking weak houses, scattering papers, and tearing pages of books. The poet shows how the wind mocks the weak and fragile, crushing both physical structures and human lives. He then advises people to become strong—physically, mentally, and emotionally—so that the wind becomes a friend rather than an enemy. The poem conveys that hardships test strength and only the strong can withstand challenges. It ends on a hopeful note, praising the wind as a force that strengthens what is firm and resilient.
__________________________________________________________________________________


Literary Instruments:
___________________
Personification – wind is treated as a powerful, mischievous being
Metaphor – wind represents hardships and challenges of life
Repetition – repeated use of the word “crumbling” to emphasise destruction
Imagery – vivid images of broken houses, scattered papers, and roaring fires
Symbolism – strong houses and fires symbolise inner strength and resilience
