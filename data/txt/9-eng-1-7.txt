   Author: NCERT (Biographical Account)
______________________________


Characters:
___________________
Santosh Yadav – the first woman in the world to scale Mount Everest twice
Santosh’s Parents – traditional yet gradually supportive of her ambitions
Mohan Singh – a climber whose life Santosh saved during the Everest expedition
Yuri Sharapov – Maria Sharapova’s father who supported her tennis career
Yelena Sharapova – Maria Sharapova’s mother
Maria Sharapova – Russian tennis player and world number one
__________________________________________________________________________________


Short Summary:
___________________
The chapter presents inspiring life stories of two successful women—Santosh Yadav and Maria Sharapova—who achieved great heights through determination, discipline, and sacrifice. Santosh Yadav broke social norms to pursue education and mountaineering, eventually becoming the only woman to scale Mount Everest twice. Her courage, resilience, and concern for others during expeditions highlight her exceptional character. Maria Sharapova’s journey to the top of women’s tennis is marked by early sacrifices, intense training, and mental toughness. Despite personal hardships and separation from her family, she rose to become the world’s number one tennis player. Both stories emphasise the importance of hard work, perseverance, and unwavering focus in achieving one’s dreams.

  Poem Name: On Killing a Tree
  Poet: Gieve Patel
____________________________________________


Summary:
___________________
The poem explains that killing a tree is not an easy task and cannot be done by simply cutting its branches. A tree grows over many years, drawing strength from sunlight, air, water, and the earth. Even after being chopped, it can regenerate and grow again. To kill a tree completely, its roots must be uprooted and exposed to sun and air, leading to its slow death. The poem highlights the cruelty of destroying nature and emphasises the resilience of trees.
__________________________________________________________________________________


Literary Instruments:
___________________
Imagery – vivid description of the tree’s growth and destruction
Personification – the tree is given human qualities such as bleeding and strength
Metaphor – the tree symbolises nature’s life force
Repetition – repeated emphasis on the difficulty of killing a tree
Symbolism – roots represent the source of life and strength
Tone – critical and reflective
