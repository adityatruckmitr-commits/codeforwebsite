   Chapter Name: The Snake and the Mirror
   Author: Vaikom Muhammad Basheer
______________________________


Characters:
___________________
The Doctor – a young, poor, and self-admiring homeopath who narrates the story
The Snake – a cobra that coils around the doctor’s arm and later admires itself
Friends of the Doctor – listeners to whom the doctor narrates his experience
The Thief – an unseen character who steals the doctor’s belongings 
__________________________________________________________________________________


Short Summary:
___________________
The story is a humorous account of a frightening incident experienced by a young doctor living alone in a small rented room. One night, while admiring his appearance in the mirror and dreaming about his future, a cobra suddenly falls on him and coils around his arm. Frozen with fear, the doctor remains motionless, praying silently and fearing death. In a strange turn of events, the snake becomes distracted by its own reflection in the mirror and slithers away. Taking advantage of the moment, the doctor escapes from the room. The next day, he returns to find his room robbed of everything except his dirty vest. The story highlights human vanity, fear, and the unpredictability of life, narrated in a light-hearted and humorous tone.

  Poem Name: A Legend of the Northland
  Poet: Phoebe Cary
____________________________________________


Summary:
___________________
The poem tells a moral story set in the cold Northland about a greedy old woman who refuses to give food to Saint Peter when he asks for help. Each time she tries to bake a small cake, she finds it too large to give away. Angered by her selfishness, Saint Peter punishes her by turning her into a woodpecker. She is condemned to bore into hard wood to find her food for the rest of her life. The poem teaches the lesson that greed and lack of generosity lead to punishment.
__________________________________________________________________________________


Literary Instruments:
___________________
Ballad – a narrative poem telling a story
Rhyme scheme – regular rhyming pattern
Imagery – vivid description of the Northland and the woodpecker
Personification – human qualities given to Saint Peter and the bird
Symbolism – the woodpecker symbolises punishment for greed
Moral lesson – generosity and kindness are rewarded
