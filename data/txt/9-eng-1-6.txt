__________________________________________________________________________________


Short Summary:
___________________
The chapter is an autobiographical account of Dr A.P.J. Abdul Kalam’s early life in Rameswaram. He describes his modest upbringing in a middle-class Muslim family, highlighting the values of honesty, discipline, kindness, and generosity he inherited from his parents. The chapter narrates how Kalam earned his first wages by helping in newspaper distribution during the Second World War, which gave him a sense of pride and independence. It also focuses on his close friendships across religious boundaries and the social harmony of his childhood. Incidents of social discrimination at school are contrasted with acts of courage and reform by enlightened elders and teachers. The chapter concludes with Kalam’s father encouraging him to leave Rameswaram for higher studies, teaching the lesson that children must be allowed to grow and follow their own paths.

  Poem Name: No Men Are Foreign
  Poet: James Kirkup
____________________________________________


Summary:
___________________
The poem conveys the message that all human beings are essentially the same, regardless of nationality, race, or religion. The poet reminds readers that people across the world share the same earth, breathe the same air, and experience similar joys, hardships, love, and suffering. He warns that hatred between nations only leads to self-destruction and harms humanity as a whole. The poem strongly advocates universal brotherhood, peace, and compassion, urging people to recognise the common humanity that unites everyone.
__________________________________________________________________________________


Literary Instruments:
___________________
Repetition – repeated use of the word “Remember” to reinforce the message
Imagery – vivid images of uniforms, land, air, and human labour
Metaphor – uniforms represent artificial divisions between people
Alliteration – repetition of consonant sounds
Symbolism – earth symbolises shared human existence
Tone – persuasive and reflective
